# 14-month review: how llms.txt is announced on the web (June 2025 – July 2026)

*Published with the September 2026 monthly report, as announced in the README.*

## What was measured

Our own radar fetches files from a frozen sample. This review looks
**backwards** with a different instrument: eight monthly Common Crawl
snapshots (CC-MAIN-2025-26 … 2026-30), read as response-header metadata (WAT).
Per snapshot we scanned a fixed number of WAT files, grouped pages by
registrable domain (organisation), and counted **per organisation** who declares
something in HTTP response headers — no request to any measured domain was
made. The header series below rests on **3,643,448 pages from 2,683,451
organisations** across the eight WAT samples. A second, separate instrument —
a scan of **274,119 robots.txt files** from the same eight snapshots — feeds the
radar's directive channel (e.g. the `llms:` directive family on the observation
list); its results are not part of the table below.

Counted are **forms**, not vendors:

| Form | What it is |
|:--|:--|
| describedby → llms | `Link: <…llms.txt>; rel="describedby"` — the announcement form of the llms.txt **specification v2** |
| Vendor form | `Link: rel="llms-txt"` / `rel="llms-full-txt"` — a form found in no specification, emitted by documentation-hosting tools |
| X-Llms-Txt | a proprietary response header carrying the same pointer |
| Signposting | `rel="cite-as"` / `linkset` / `/signposting/` — the FAIR Signposting profile of scholarly repositories (a different standard, counted separately) |
| Markdown alternate | `Link: rel="alternate"; type="text/markdown"` — a Markdown twin of the page |

## What the series says

1. **No demonstrably organic adoption of an llms.txt announcement form.** The
   vendor form appears in February 2026 out of nothing and moves in lockstep
   with the proprietary header — the signature of one tool being rolled out to
   many hosted sites, not of many operators deciding. Even the growth of the
   *specification* form since June 2026 carries a uniform tool signature in the
   samples we read.
2. **Signposting is the only stable, long-lived series** (96 → 140
   organisations) — a different ecosystem (scholarly repositories) answering the
   same question, and the reason it is counted separately.
3. **Markdown alternates are the fastest-growing form** (0 → 100) and the one
   open question: whether this is one tool or several is not decidable from
   headers alone.

## How to read it — and how not

- These are **counts of organisations in a sample of a third-party corpus**,
  useful for ratios and timing, **never** for adoption shares of "the web".
  The radar's own shares live in the README and in `data/`.
- A correction is part of this series: our first version of the describedby
  signal counted the relation without checking its target and therefore
  measured Signposting for fourteen months. It was fixed on 22 August 2026 and
  all eight snapshots were re-run; the corrected numbers are the ones above.
- Vendors are deliberately not named: what is measured is the form on the
  wire. The same form emitted tomorrow by a second tool would still be counted
  correctly; a company name would not.
